One Island

Everything so far was drawn volume by volume. From here on, the series is one physical place: Paxos, modelled once in Blender, shot like a film set, and inked as a Franco-Belgian album. This first pass decides the island itself: what it must hold, what shape it is, and how it becomes terrain.

1Paxos is where every volume happens (Volume IX perhaps excepted).
2The whole island is pre-visualised in Blender. The Round, the circular harbour and the other key buildings get detailed models.
3Each panel starts as a Blender pre-vis render, then becomes a Franco-Belgian comic image.
4There's one shared asset sheet for the whole series, plus a sheet for each volume.
  1. Rough mapFive layouts drawn from real elevation data. E, Dependency Spine, is chosen.
  2. Painted mapAn image model fills in the chosen map: vegetation, town fabric, coves, cliffs.
  3. Island blockoutThe heightmap displaces a plane in Blender; site empties come from the JSON; you sculpt.
  4. Hero architectureThe Round, the cothon, the citadel and the granary are hand-modelled. Tripo makes the props.
  5. Shot pre-visOne camera per panel: a clay render plus a line pass.
  6. Comic passLigne claire over the pre-vis, checked against the asset sheets.

Everything on this page can be rebuilt. The maps come from tools/island_maps.py, which also writes a 16-bit heightmap and a site list for each option to maps/. Nothing from the earlier volume art is assumed to survive, except the Volume V chamber and port decisions (D02, D05), which the maps honour.

2Gazetteer: what the island must hold

These are the 23 sites of the chosen map, E, numbered in the order you meet them walking from the north-west corner. Every site belongs to exactly one volume. Where volumes used to share a place, it's rebuilt in each volume's own zone: IV has its lantern harbour, V its merchant quays, VII a walled harbour of its own, and VIII a guild hall instead of borrowing the Round. That's what lets the walk read the papers in dependency order. Elevation and distance to the sea are enforced when a site is placed, and sightline rules are tested by casting rays across the terrain. Maps A–D predate this list and use the older 16 shared sites in the legend above them.

3Island form: five layouts

Maps A–D share an 8 × 5.5 km world; E needs 11 × 8 km to fit the Raft islet. All five use the same sites and the same rules; only the landform changes. The land is coloured in elevation bands (0, 20, 70, 140, 220, 300 and 380 m), and the blue lines offshore trace the 5, 13 and 24 m depths. Roads are found by least-cost paths, so they climb the way a mule track would.

A

Crescent

A horseshoe of hills wrapped round one sheltered bay that opens to the north.

Where things fall

The harbour town and cothon sit at the bottom of the bay, the calmest water on the island. A spur on the east side of the bay holds the headland city. The Round sits on the southern saddle, looking down into the bay on one side and out to the open sea on the other. The west horn rises to the oracle's mountain. The east horn ends at a strait with an islet beyond it.

  • Harbour, Round and citadel all face one bay, so one establishing shot covers Volumes V and VII.
  • The bay reads instantly as "the port of Paxos" on a cover.
  • The Volume I hamlets pass the sightline test only because a hill hides one from another. On one continuous arc of land, they don't feel cut off.
  • The Volume IV strait is only the gap to a small islet, not a split between two assemblies.
  • The near-symmetrical silhouette looks designed rather than natural.

B

Twin Lobes

previous choice · fallback

Two hill masses joined by a sand causeway that the winter sea covers.

Where things fall

The west lobe is the civic island. The oracle's mountain rises at its heart. To the north a peninsula runs out to the citadel hill. The Round sits in the col on the peninsula's neck, between the citadel hill and the western highland, so the ground rises on two sides of it and falls away to the sea on the other two. The harbour town and cothon lie below it to the south-east, facing the water between the lobes, and the Statue Walk climbs about 650 m up to the gate. The banquet house stands on a graded garden terrace 160 m due east of the Round, below the east gate. The east lobe is farmland: hamlets, the olive press, the granary plain and the guild ridge. Between the lobes runs a sand causeway about 600 m long, its crest 1.5–2 m above calm water.

  • Volume IV's title becomes geography. The causeway is dry in the calm season, but its crest is low enough for winter seas to break over it. In a storm the island becomes two assemblies, each deciding the other is dead.
  • Volume I's hamlets sit on different lobes, cut off by the causeway, with beacons across the south bay.
  • The Round sits in a measured saddle, with sea glimpsed through both sides of the col and the cothon below. That's D02's "windswept saddle above the sea", and it passes every sightline test.
  • The silhouette is instantly recognisable on a cover, and it can't be mistaken for a real Greek island.
  • It has the most land (about 15 km²), so there's the most terrain to dress.
  • The causeway has to be modelled in two states, dry and awash.
  • The town is squeezed onto the slope between the cothon and the Round, so it climbs the hill rather than spreading out.

C

Caldera

A broken volcanic ring around a drowned crater, with a smoking cone in the middle.

Where things fall

The ring opens to the north-west. The harbour and cothon shelter inside the lagoon at the eastern side of that entrance, and the Round sits on the rim just above them. The oracle lives on the cone, whose vapours give the Delphic trance a physical cause. A detached piece of the rim makes the strait. The outer slopes carry the hamlets and the granary. The steep inner walls are the ledger cliffs.

  • It has the most spectacular silhouette, and the lagoon gives the calmest harbour water of the four.
  • Volume III's oracle and Volume VIII's cliffs come with the landform, so you don't have to invent them.
  • It looks like Santorini, which fights D05's Delos-type trading port.
  • The inner walls are too steep for the town–agora–Round walk to feel ordinary, and the Statue Walk is short.
  • The volcano steals every wide shot.

D

Ridge Spine

One long limestone ridge running north-west to south-east, with olive slopes to the east and sheer cliffs to the west.

Where things fall

This option is modelled on the real Paxos (Paxoi): a long ridge, a round bay at the northern tip like Lakka, and an islet to the south across a strait, like Antipaxos. The harbour town and cothon sit on the sheltered east coast under the citadel's headland. The Round sits on the spine above them. The oracle's cave opens in the western sea cliffs, as the real island's caves do.

  • It's grounded in the actual island the paper is named after.
  • It's the simplest terrain to build: one ridge and two coasts.
  • It has the least memorable silhouette.
  • The real Paxos is in the Ionian Sea, while the paper says Aegean, so the resemblance may be more distraction than help.
  • The layout is linear, so the ends of the island are far from the town.

I and III share the first lobe: the hamlets in valleys either side of the summit, the oracle on top. IV is the neck, with its lantern harbour, drummers and causeway. V is the Round in a col between two knolls, above its merchant quays and town. Past that, II (the besieged headland city and its camps) sits beside VI (the granary plain), as in the graph. Then come VII (a citadel above its own harbour) and VIII (the guild hall, the lock-house ridge and the ledger cliffs). IX is the Raft monks' islet across a strait.

  • The walk is a reading order. A check tests all nine dependencies in the graph. Every site of a volume lies further along the axis than every site of each volume it builds on. The graph's five bands (I+III, IV, V, II+VI, VII+VIII+IX) also follow one another without overlapping.
  • No site is shared, so each volume can be shot in its own zone without the others' buildings in frame.
  • Volume IX gets its departure in the picture: the monastery is in view across the strait but cut off from the rest.
  • Volume IV's storm still splits the island in two, at the neck.
  • This is the biggest world (11 × 8 km, about 18.5 km² of land), and there are more buildings: three harbours instead of one, and a guild hall.
  • Map order is dependency order, not story order. II sits after V here, but ERAS.md still puts its siege in the first phase.
  • The Round, its quays and the Statue Walk's descent are pinned and graded by hand to pass the sightline check, so the middle of the island is the most "engineered" ground.
  • The four later volumes share one lobe, so their zones are tighter than the early ones.

G02 · Island form

Chosen: E (16 Sep 2026), so the geography carries the reading order. Before that, B was chosen because the hardest volumes to move (I and IV) get their separation from the landform itself; E keeps that with its NW lobe and causeway neck. Earlier reasoning: Why B: it's the only form where the hardest volumes to move (I and IV) get their separation from the landform itself, and every option passes the sightline tests except the Caldera's "sea on both sides", so the choice mostly comes down to story. The shape also stays open to change. Moving a lobe, lengthening the causeway or sharpening the col is a one-line edit in the generator.

4The circular harbour

Volume IV needs a circular harbour with seven quays and a captain at each, where "the lantern goes round the harbour for ever". Volume V has locked a Delos-type port D05. Carthage had exactly the building that satisfies both: an ordinary merchant harbour leading into a round naval harbour (the cothon) with an island at its centre. On maps A–D the cothon is carved into the one shared port. On the chosen map E, every site belongs to one volume, so the cothon is Volume IV's lantern harbour at the neck. V gets plain merchant quays below the Round, and VII a walled harbour below its citadel.

G03 · Circular harbour

A: one harbour serves Volume IV (the lantern), Volume V (the voyages) and Volume VII (the inquisitors landing), and it's the model worth detailing. The island's lantern tower is where the Volume IV lantern lives. B: Volume IV gets a place of its own, but there are two harbours to model. C: a Lakka-style bay with seven timber jetties is cheaper to build but less monumental. Chosen: B (16 Sep 2026, with map E): the cothon is Volume IV's own, and V and VII get harbours of their own.

5Scale, time and Volume IX

Real islands for scale

Delos 3.4 km² · Paxoi 19 km² · Santorini 76 km². The drafts come out at 10–19 km², with a 50 m Round. Walking from the harbour to the Round takes 10–15 minutes uphill, which is long enough for a Statue Walk.

What the camera needs

In an island-wide shot, a 50 m Round is about 1% of the frame. That's fine for a map and too small for a cover. Covers will use a three-quarter view from the saddle, not the whole island.

One generation, four phases

The whole series spans about 40 island years in a Hellenistic, Delos-type free port. That's roughly the papers' own span, 1978–2014. 1 · Before the Round (I–IV): hamlets, the siege, the oracle, the causeway; scaffolding in the saddle. 2 · The Parliament (V–VI): the Round finished, a few statues, new storehouses. 3 · Citadel & guilds (VII–VIII): the citadel above its own harbour, the Italian guild hall and lock-houses, cut cliffs, weathered marble. 4 · Reformation (IX). Buildings are only ever added. The terrain, roads, costume, ships and palette never change, and together they make the common asset sheet. The present-day dig is a narrator's frame, not a modelled era.

G04 · World scale

At compact scale the hamlets can see each other, which breaks Volume I. At large scale the Round disappears from every establishing shot, and there's twice the terrain to dress. Chosen: C at 11 × 8 km, because E needs room for the Raft islet. Land is about 19 km², against B's 15.

G05 · Time across volumes

A: one .blend file, with small additive era collections switched on per shot, so Volume VIII's Round is Volume V's with a weathered material. Chosen, with the eras close together. B: each volume is simpler on its own, but the copies drift apart the first time the terrain gets fixed in only one of them.

G06 · Volume IX

A keeps "sailed north" and the break with Paxos, and the coast can be glimpsed from the north cape in Volume V or VIII. B is cheap to model but makes the monastery a neighbour rather than a departure. C contradicts the premise of the volume as written. Chosen: B (16 Sep 2026, with option E): the monks withdraw to an islet across a strait at the south-east end of the dependency axis. It stays a departure because nothing on the main island connects to it. "Sailed north" becomes "crossed to the island".

6From map to Blender

The maps and the terrain come from one source. Stage 3 doesn't have to trace an image: each option already has a heightmap that displaces a plane at true scale, plus a list of site positions to drop in as empties.

Hand-built

The terrain (a displaced plane, then sculpted), the Great Round, the cothon, the headland city and citadel, the granary storehouses and the lock-houses. These need true scale, clean topology for ruin variants, and consistent gates and doors across volumes.

Tripo

Statues for the Statue Walk, amphorae, ships and skiffs, the hourglass, ledgers and other furniture. Image-to-3D produces one closed mesh from a single view with no real scale. That's fine for props and wrong for an 8 km island or a building people walk through.

Painted map (stage 2)

The chosen SVG, at the heightmap's framing, goes to the image model as a layout guide. It adds vegetation, field patterns, town fabric and cliff texture. That painting then becomes the texture reference for stage 3.

G07 · Where the 3D work lives

The work already lives in its own repo, grand-paxos, away from the 911 MB published site. Chosen: B. The generator, maps and page are versioned. .blend, .glb and renders stay out of Git, and renders go to R2 as in the old repo. A would version the scene files too, but they grow quickly and don't diff.
